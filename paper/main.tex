\begin{abstract}
We describe our system for the AKBC 2026 shared task on closed-book
knowledge base construction. Our final system is a single pretrained-only
backbone, Gemma-3-27B-pt, queried entirely in a raw text-completion
format, with 20 candidates per query, relation-specific gated
aggregation, and no arbiter; it uses 27B of the 32B parameter budget and
scores a row-averaged Macro F1 of 0.650 on validation and 0.6493 on the
official test set, against 0.313 for the official baseline. Its
predecessor---two deliberately different prompts over Mistral Small 3.2
24B with a cross-family 8B arbiter, 31.8B in total---scores 0.6247. Most
of the paper presents a mechanism study over three backbones and roughly
thirty controlled experiments, organized around three results. First, how
reliably a backbone recalls the value stored for an entity label
dominated every intervention we tried: each backbone change moved the
score more than all our prompt, sampling, and selection experiments
combined, and the winning backbone is a pretrained checkpoint in a
completion format, not an instruct model in a chat format. Second,
exemplar style moves retrieval and instruction text does not---two
in-prompt exemplars demonstrating a verbalized recall move the answer
mode by $+6$ rows, while the same request as an instruction moves
nothing---but instructions do move \emph{rejection}, the discarding of
already-generated candidates. Third, validation gains transferred when
the change was gated (applied only where a condition holds) \emph{and}
the gate consulted information from outside the model; a
mechanism-derived but ungated rescoring gained $+2$ validation rows and
lost a test row.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The LM-KBC shared task series \citep{lmkbc2023,lmkbc2024} probes how much
factual knowledge language models can turn into knowledge base entries:
given a subject and a relation, a system must output \emph{all} correct
object values---possibly none, one, or hundreds---under closed-book
conditions: no retrieval, no external corpora, no fine-tuning, and at most
32B neural parameters at inference.

Under these conditions the only controls are at inference time: model
choice, prompts, sampling, and aggregation over samples. Our development
proceeded in three stages: a Qwen3.5-27B thinking system (test 0.571), a
Mistral Small 3.2 system with a second prompt and a cross-family 8B
arbiter (0.6247), and a single pretrained-only Gemma-3-27B backbone in a
completion format (0.6493). Alongside the system we report the controlled
experiments behind each decision, including submissions whose test scores
give us what validation cannot: isolated ablations on the hidden set.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Backbone recall reliability dominates.} A 12-row probe
    (30 GPU-minutes per model) ranked backbones consistently with
    downstream quality in every deployment decision we evaluated, and
    both backbone changes beat all our prompt and selection work combined
    (Sec.~\ref{sec:screening}).
  \item \textbf{Answers commit early} to the first concrete value they
    generate (Sec.~\ref{sec:anchor}), across backbones.
  \item \textbf{Exemplar style moves retrieval; instruction text does
    not} (Sec.~\ref{sec:exemplar}); instructions move rejection. The
    effect is conditional on recall reliability and on post-training
    (Sec.~\ref{sec:conditions}).
  \item \textbf{Within one model we found no usable selection signal};
    across prompts and families we did (Sec.~\ref{sec:ensemble}).
  \item \textbf{Validation gains transfer when the change is gated and
    the gate looks outside the model} (Sec.~\ref{sec:transfer}).
  \item \textbf{The residual errors are consistent with incorrect values
    learned from training data}, and are associated with the
    instruct-plus-chat setting (Sec.~\ref{sec:associations}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Task and Constraints}

The 2026 task covers six relations over 478 validation and 477 test
subject--relation pairs: two set-valued relations that may be empty
(\texttt{countryLandBordersCountry},
\texttt{companyTradesAtStockExchange}), one single-valued string relation
that may be empty (\texttt{personHasCityOfDeath}), one large-set relation
(\texttt{awardWonBy}), and two numeric relations (\texttt{hasArea},
\texttt{hasCapacity}) scored at 5\% relative tolerance. Gold values come
from Wikidata (areas \texttt{P2046}, capacities \texttt{P1083}), with
alias lists accepted for strings. We report the official ``All
Relations'' score throughout: a Macro F1 averaged over \emph{rows}, not
relations, so the four 100-row relations dominate it. Systems are
closed-book and capped at 32B inference parameters.

\section{System Description}
\label{sec:system}

\subsection{Final system}

The final system generates with Gemma-3-27B-pt (the pretrained
checkpoint, Q8\_0, served by \texttt{llama.cpp} at a pinned revision),
one model and no arbiter, 27B of the 32B budget. Every relation is
queried in a \emph{raw text-completion format}---a record- or
infobox-style prefix that the model continues, with no chat template and
no instruction turn---with 20 candidates per query at temperature 0.6,
top-$p$ 0.95 and deterministic per-candidate seeds, as in the earlier
systems.

Aggregation is relation-specific and, in every case, \emph{gated}: a rule
applies only where an explicit support condition holds and falls back to
a default otherwise. Numeric candidates are clustered at 5\% relative
tolerance and \texttt{dominant\_cluster} predicts the median of the
best-supported cluster. \texttt{personHasCityOfDeath} takes a surface
majority with a minimum-vote gate, predicting empty below it.
\texttt{companyTradesAtStockExchange} folds surface variants---case
variants and acronyms that expand to a co-occurring long form (NYSE
$\rightarrow$ New York Stock Exchange)---into a single count, repairing
vote splits that would drop majority answers below its frequency
threshold ($\ge0.5$), and abstains on an empty majority. \texttt{countryLandBordersCountry} and \texttt{awardWonBy} use
recall-oriented thresholds ($\ge0.3$, $\ge0.1$) with the same abstention.

\textbf{Reproducibility.} Our submissions appear on the official
Codabench leaderboard under the team name \texttt{acro-sasaki}. Code,
configurations, builders, the step-by-step recipe
(\texttt{docs/V4\_REPRODUCE.md}), and per-experiment records are at
\url{https://github.com/acrosasaki-rgb/llm-knowledge-task-2026}. Model
revisions, container digests, and the dataset commit are pinned; each
prediction file is a deterministic function of persisted candidate sets
and a builder script (SHA-256 verified).

\subsection{Predecessor system}

The mechanism experiments of Sec.~\ref{sec:anatomy}, and all our
per-component test measurements, come from the Mistral-era system that
preceded it. It samples 20 candidates per prompt from Mistral Small 3.2
24B under \emph{two} prompts of deliberately different style for
\texttt{hasArea}: a \textbf{recall prompt} with no few-shot examples but
two in-prompt exemplars demonstrating a labeled recall format
(\texttt{Entity:}, \texttt{Memory:}\ ``I recall that the Wikipedia
infobox lists X's total area as Y'', \texttt{Unit:}, \texttt{Answer:}),
and a \textbf{grounding prompt} with five bare-JSON few-shot examples and
an instruction naming the recall target (Wikidata
\texttt{P2046}/\texttt{P1083}). \texttt{hasCapacity} adds a third prompt
at zero additional parameter-count cost: the same 24B model queried as
raw text completion.
When two prompts disagree by more than 5\%, one aggregated arbiter
decision---the mode over 20 samples of an unrelated-family
Llama-3.1-8B---breaks the tie, switching to the secondary answer only if
the arbiter matches it and not the primary one. The arbiter never
introduces a value neither prompt proposed, but an incorrect switch can
still lose a row. Total: $24\text{B}+8\text{B}\approx31.8\text{B}$.

\section{Anatomy of Inference-Time Interventions}
\label{sec:anatomy}

All experiments change one stated condition; numeric testbeds are
\texttt{hasArea} and \texttt{hasCapacity} (100 validation rows $\times$
20 candidates per prompt). \textbf{Oracle} is the number of rows whose
candidate set contains at least one within-tolerance value; the
\textbf{mode} row count is what \texttt{dominant\_cluster} selects. The
\textbf{anchor} is the first concrete value for the queried quantity in a
candidate.

Our Qwen-era system plateaued after nine intervention experiments
(prompts, temperature, output routes, thinking budgets, judge models,
log-probability confidence) whose combined deployable gain was under
$+0.01$. A 12-row probe of its wrong rows across candidate models
(Table~\ref{tab:probe}) showed Mistral Small 3.2 answering 11 of the 12
somewhere in its candidate set; regenerating the pipeline on Mistral with
nothing else changed moved test 0.571$\rightarrow$0.595.

The second change was larger and reversed an earlier decision. Gemma~4
31B Instruct, queried through its chat template, had the weakest
\texttt{hasArea} oracle we measured (43/100) and we closed that lineage
early. Probing the \emph{pretrained} Gemma-3-27B checkpoint in a
completion format instead gave the best profile of any configuration we
tested---oracle 94, mode 84 on \texttt{hasArea}, borders 0.986---and a
system built on it took test $0.625\rightarrow0.649$. It also supplies
values no prompt reached on Mistral: secondary stock listings that five
Mistral prompt variants returned with zero votes (\v{C}EZ on Warsaw,
Questerre on Oslo, Aisin on Nagoya) appear with 7--13 votes out of 20.
This was the dominant empirical factor in our experiments: closed-book
quality is set by which label-to-value pairings pretraining left intact
and by whether the prompting format can reach them.

\subsection{Answers Commit Early}
\label{sec:anchor}

In persisted reasoning traces of the Qwen-era system, the answer value
first appears at a median 17\% of the trace, in 95--98\% of candidates
inside a retrieval template (``Commonly cited: X''); the remaining 80\%
of the trace never dislodges it. The verification text that follows is
consistent with post-hoc justification rather than independent checking:
fabricated Wikidata Q-IDs and cross-language ``confirmations'' agree with
whatever the anchor was, and wrong candidates carry \emph{more} of this
apparatus than correct ones (6.9 vs.\ 4.2 source mentions). Early
commitment replicates on Gemma~4 31B and Qwen3.6; the confabulated
verification does not. Extended thinking is thus an \emph{exploration}
aid: it raises the oracle where recall is unreliable (Qwen $+9$ rows,
Gemma $+7$) and has no measurable effect on Mistral or Magistral.

The single largest prompt effect we found---\texttt{hasArea} mode
$72\rightarrow78$, transferring to test as $0.70\rightarrow0.75$---looked
at first like an instruction effect: a step-structured expert prompt,
adapted from a style published by another participant, beat our grounding
instruction by 6 rows. The $2{\times}2$ comparison
(Table~\ref{tab:grid}) shows the instruction text is nearly inert. What
matters is the \emph{exemplars}: with two in-prompt
exemplars demonstrating a one-sentence verbalized recall (``I recall that
the Wikipedia infobox lists Luxembourg's total area as 2586.4\,km$^2$''),
99\% of candidates reproduce the verbalization and the mode moves $+6$;
with five bare-JSON few-shot exemplars, 0\% verbalize---the same
procedure requested as instruction text is silently ignored---and the
mode does not move. Iterating the wording (persona, fixed phrase,
provenance vocabulary) changed nothing, while reproducing the labeled
structure with our own labels recovered the full effect.

\textbf{Instructions move rejection.} Instruction text was not inert
everywhere: it moved the \emph{discarding} of already-generated
candidates. Verifying each \texttt{awardWonBy} candidate with three
yes/no votes and dropping only unanimous-no candidates raised precision
$0.241\rightarrow0.280$ and F1 $0.172\rightarrow0.186$; the yes votes
carried no information, so the usable signal was one-sided. A digit-check
step inside an otherwise inert step-by-step procedure likewise cut votes
for order-of-magnitude twin values from 38 to 32 with the oracle
unchanged. Instructions act after retrieval, on which candidates survive.

\subsection{The Exemplar Effect Is Conditional}
\label{sec:conditions}

Verbalized recall is a \emph{focusing} intervention---it sharpens the
anchor distribution around the most frequently recalled value (oracle
drops $89\rightarrow84$ as the mode improves). Its sign therefore follows
how reliable the model's recall already is, in mirror image to thinking:
thinking explores, gaining $+9$ rows where recall is unreliable (Qwen)
and nothing where it is reliable (Mistral); verbalized recall does the
opposite, zero or negative on Qwen and on Mistral's weakest relation
(\texttt{hasCapacity} oracle $60\rightarrow47$) but $+6$ on
\texttt{hasArea}. In Magistral (Mistral's RL-tuned sibling) the effect
disappears: it copies the verbalized form at 99.9\% compliance---in its
thinking channel, in a scripted thinking channel, and in the answer
body---yet its anchor distribution does not move in any of the three
(70--72), suggesting that RL post-training may decouple response style
from retrieval.

\subsection{We Find No Usable Within-Model Selection Signal in the
Features We Tested}
\label{sec:ensemble}

Within one model's candidate set, minority correct values were
indistinguishable from minority wrong ones on every feature we measured
(votes, dispersion, trace features, log-probabilities; base rate 8\%),
which is why judges, confidence weighting, and consensus schemes failed
in our experiments. What did extract signal is \emph{disagreement between
prompts of different style}: on the $\sim$25 rows where the recall and
grounding prompts disagree by more than 5\%, one aggregated decision from
an unrelated-family 8B model resolves the tie, giving
\texttt{hasArea} $78\rightarrow80$ and \texttt{hasCapacity}
$26\rightarrow28$ on validation; among the four \texttt{hasArea} rows it
changed, arbitration corrected three and harmed one. Controls locate the
conditions: same-style prompt pairs produce too little disagreement to
arbitrate; promoting minority clusters by the arbiter's mode is
\emph{negative}---an 8B cannot overrule a 24B mode, only break ties; and
a weak arbiter (Ministral-8B, same family, mode accuracy 23 vs.\ Llama's
53 of 100) gains nothing. The isolated v3$\rightarrow$v4 ablation---four
capacity rows changed, nothing else---confirms the transfer on test:
capacity F1 $0.204\rightarrow0.214$.

Our submissions give per-component test measurements
(Table~\ref{tab:transfer}). The components that transferred share two
properties: each is \emph{gated}, touching only rows that meet an
explicit condition, and each gate consults information from outside the
model---an unrelated-family vote, or the gold alias structure that makes
two surface forms one count. The exemplar effect is the apparent
exception: it changes what the backbone retrieves rather than
adjudicating between candidates.

The components that did not transfer are fits to validation row
distributions: the best of six capacity configurations (winner's curse),
an increment to an already-saturated arbiter, and a city pipeline whose
$+11$-row gain rested on the rate at which split-vote rows happen to be
correct-mode. The most instructive case is a tolerance-geometry rescoring
we deployed in v9--v10. Two values whose ratio is below
$1.05/0.95\approx1.105$ admit a single prediction correct for either, so
each answer can be shifted inside its own $\pm5\%$ window to also cover a
runner-up cluster. It follows from the metric geometry, has no tuned
constants, and gained $+1$ to $+2$ rows across every prompt pairing and
arbiter seed we tested, yet it cost a row on test (\texttt{hasArea}
$0.800\rightarrow0.790$), because it moves \emph{every} row rather than
only rows meeting a condition; we removed it in v11. Later gated experiments failed the
second criterion the same way: certificates computed from the same
model's internal consistency (unit agreement, answer-blind priming)
admitted confident errors and lost rows. A 100-row validation set may not
reliably expose this form of overfitting; in our experiments, whether a
component is gated and whether its gate looks outside the model predicted
transfer better than effect size or the presence of a mechanism-level
rationale.

\subsection{Residual Errors Look Like Values Learned From the Corpus}
\label{sec:associations}

The residual errors we examined are not reasoning failures. Corfu (gold
626\,km$^2$) is answered as 592.9 by every Mistral-family instruct
configuration, 593 by Qwen3.5, and 580 by Gemma: stable, model-specific
wrong values that survive every prompt. Three observations support the
hypothesis that they were learned from training data. First,
\emph{turnover across generations}: Qwen3.6 repairs 7 of Qwen3.5's wrong
rows and breaks 11 new ones with nothing else changed, and the union
oracle across generations reaches 89/100. Second, \emph{unrelated
training lineages solve mainstream-broken rows}: llm-jp-4-32b and Aya
Expanse are far weaker overall yet each partially solves Corfu, which no
mainstream-corpus model in our set solves. Third, \emph{the
instruct-plus-chat setting is associated with a stronger incorrect
anchor}: under raw completion Mistral 3.1 Base scores 10/12-any and
7/12-mode against its own instruct's 9/4, the gap replicates on
Llama-3.1-8B (mode accuracy 60 vs.\ 53 of 100), and the largest instance
is the Gemma lineage of Sec.~\ref{sec:screening}. These comparisons
change weights and prompting format together; what they establish is that
the accessible ceiling is a property of the backbone and its prompting
format, not of prompt engineering within one setting.

\section{Related Work}

Probing factual knowledge in LMs goes back to LAMA
\citep{petroni2019lama}; the LM-KBC series \citep{lmkbc2023,lmkbc2024}
extends it to KB construction with unconstrained cardinalities.
Self-consistency \citep{wang2023selfconsistency} aggregates sampled
answers by majority vote and ReWiSe \citep{albertroulhac2025rewise} tuned
it relation-wise; we extend the line to empty answer sets, numeric
tolerance, surface-form folding, and cross-family arbitration.
\citet{sprague2025cot} find chain-of-thought helps reasoning but not
knowledge recall; we refine this for recall tasks, where exemplar style
moves retrieval although procedures and instructions do not.
\citet{ma2026parametric} report prompting moves parametric knowledge
access by 1--2\% and RL fine-tuning by $\sim$10\%; our exemplar effect
($+6$\% on the affected relation) sits between, and fine-tuning is
prohibited here. \citet{numbers2025know} find probe-recoverable numeric
knowledge exceeds what models verbalize, one reading of our oracle--mode
gaps; \citet{kalyan2021fermi} and \citet{park2022measurements} document
the magnitude and unit fragilities our decimal-rule and unit-merge
components target; and grounding instructions instantiate ``According
to\ldots'' cues \citep{weller2024according}.

\section{Conclusion}

Our final system is a single pretrained-only 27B backbone queried in a
completion format with gated aggregation (validation 0.650, test 0.6493);
the two-prompt, arbiter-ensembled 24B system it replaced scores 0.6247.
The most reliable finding behind that progression is that backbone recall
reliability, together with a prompting format that can reach it,
outweighed every prompt-level intervention we tested, and that a
half-hour probe ranked candidates well enough to act on.

Two rules earned their place. Choose exemplars, not instructions, to
change what a model retrieves; instruction text in our experiments moved
only the rejection of already-generated candidates. And before deploying
what a small validation set endorses, ask whether the component is gated
and whether its gate consults anything outside the model---those that
failed this test cost us rows even with a clean mechanism-level
rationale. Both rules are bounded by what we measured: three backbones
and one task edition.

\section*{Limitations}

The mechanism findings rest on two model families in depth (Qwen,
Mistral) with single-seed generation for most prompts; seed replications
exist only for the exemplar, arbitration, and rescoring results. The
final Gemma-pt system is the least ablated configuration here: built and
submitted within one day, its per-component contributions are not
separated on test and its one regression (city,
$0.510\rightarrow0.490$) is unexplained. The 12-row probe is stratified
on one system's failures, and its ranking is validated on the deployment
decisions we took, not a held-out model set. Oracle and mode counts on
100-row relations carry $\pm1$--$2$ row noise, which bounds several
negative results. Every base-versus-instruct and
pretrained-versus-instruct comparison changes weights and prompting
format together, which we flag rather than resolve, and the account of
residual errors is observational. Aggregation thresholds are
validation-tuned, and the transfer criterion of Sec.~\ref{sec:transfer}
comes from one task edition.
Finally, submissions v6--v9 included a second 8B arbiter that put the
ensemble at $\approx$39.8B, over the 32B cap; the violation was ours,
found in a self-audit, and v10 removed it at no test cost. Their test
numbers should be read as at-risk under the cap rule; v11 and v12 are
compliant.
